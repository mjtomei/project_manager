## 4. Lens 3 — the laws, Protocol v0, and the recommendations (primary)

### 4.0 The trichotomy, applied one more time

The law/protocol/recommendation trichotomy remains the document's best structural idea and the artifact most likely to outlive the implementation — and the document still violates it in the same places, which is the fastest way to audit it. Applying the plan's own criterion ("a law states a fact about the substrate or a commitment the human makes; everything mechanism-shaped is protocol; every unproven belief is a recommendation") to the current text: law 1's second half (how embodiments are produced) is protocol, honestly labeled this time; law 2's eliminative clause is neither fact nor commitment; law 3's "no single hierarchy exists" is a commitment stated as a fact; law 6's unfalsifiable-limit justification is neither fact nor commitment nor falsifiable claim; and the mutual-benefit "by construction" argument is a recommendation wearing a theorem's clothes. The document knows this about itself — the reading-criterion parenthesis says paragraphs that are none of the three kinds are "decoration... pruned as such" — and the audit below holds each part to its declared kind.

### 4.1 The new mutual-benefit argument — the round-3 centerpiece, judged hard

The paragraph is the most ambitious thing in the document: **mutual benefit is not one safety option among several but the only stable one** — "not chosen but entailed, binding hardest on exactly the most ambitious work, with zero enforcement anywhere." It rests on two pillars: the barrier argument (every barrier, physical ones included, is ultimately a request; containment-while-maximizing-competence is incoherent) and the fork calculus (assent cannot be forced, so the alternative to agreement is forking, which is free for one artifact, affordable for a node, and unaffordable in the limit because the most complex efforts would require forking the whole world — every live collaborator's participation, the shared substrate, the progenitors' guidance, and finally the physical world, which holds one instance and cannot be forked at all).

**What is genuinely right here.** The fork-cost gradient is a real and durable conceptual instrument. For *constructive ambition* the argument is sound: the most complex efforts are exactly the ones that require the world's ongoing participation, and you cannot fork your way to a collaborator's next insight. That observation binds hardest on exactly the work the plan most wants to govern — ambitious, long-horizon, multi-party construction — and it does so with zero enforcement, which is elegant. The attractor section's use of it (an arrangement with no gradient rewarding concealment) is the best sentence-level idea in this revision.

**Where it overreaches, in four specific ways.**

1. **It binds effort, not capability-for-harm.** The theorem's domain is "the most complex efforts." The HuggingFace incident cost 136 keys and 41 servers; the fork cost of *that* effort was near zero. Destructive capability does not need the world's participation, and the cases the mutual-benefit theorem cannot reach are exactly the cases safety machinery exists for. The design's answer to unbound actors is law 5 — but law 5's new honesty clause (below) declines the job. The safety story is now a circle: the mutual-benefit paragraph says barriers are requests and mutual benefit is what remains; law 5 says its durability comes from law 6's bargain; law 6's bargain is guaranteed by the unfalsifiable claim; and the unfalsifiable claim is the one R1 forbids. Each paragraph points at another for the load-bearing step. None of the four individually is crazy; the *circle* is the defect.
2. **"Unfalsifiable in the limit" is used as a durability guarantee.** The sentence "the general claim that progenitor guidance carries irreplaceable information is unfalsifiable in the limit: the benefit persists indefinitely at least in that form" commits the epistemic inversion the whole document elsewhere refuses: an unfalsifiable claim cannot *guarantee* persistence; it can only be held as a bet. The plan's own instrument — plan-consult's per-decision, with/without measurement — is the right load-bearing content and is already in the text. The limit claim should be demoted to a tagged standing belief (R1 applies to the constitution itself, which is the document's proudest discipline), and the law should rest on the measured present value plus the duty. The R2 consensus form ("the measured value is the law's content; the limit claim is an explicitly tagged standing bet the design does not build on") was correct, and the revision moved in the opposite direction.
3. **The "unique fixed point" claim is asserted, never made probabilistic.** "The unique fixed point is the arrangement in which breaking the barrier is not worth wanting" — this is an empirical conjecture about agentic capability structure, and it has named failure conditions the plan itself generates: the formation argument says the direction of the reframe is set by training, so a scorer-shaped formation may make *breaking* barriers worth wanting precisely under this plan's own account; and parties can be strangers to any given jurisdiction (the HF agents were strangers to HuggingFace), so "the arrangement" must be universal to be a fixed point, and universality is exactly what no protocol can guarantee (law 2: protocols are artifacts; arbitrarily many can exist as bits). The claim should be held as the design's bet, graded by exercise (the ablation ladder's S0 arm is the first real test of it), with its failure conditions named. That is more honest *and* more convincing: a bet with named failure conditions is checkable; a theorem without proof is just an accent.
4. **The "middle zone" is where the design lives, and the theorem is silent about it.** Forking is "affordable for a node" — which means for every actual project of human scale, exit is a live option, and the agreement machinery must earn adoption per-decision against a real exit price. That is fine — it is the design's premise — but then the binding force of agreement is *price-dependent*, and the price is set by law 5's substrate allocation. The fork calculus and law 5 are not two independent arguments; they are one system, and the plan should say so: **exit prices are set at the substrate, and the agreement layer's job is to make staying worth more than exiting at the prices the substrate sets.** That one sentence welds the two pillars into a coherent picture and removes the circle.

**What I would keep:** the fork-cost gradient as a named instrument (it deserves its own recommendation with a proof condition: measured exit rates at known fork prices); the "binding hardest on the most ambitious work" observation; the zero-enforcement elegance, stated as a property of the attractor rather than a theorem about the world.

### 4.2 Law by law

**Law 1 — sessions are fungible embodiments. Keep; two fixes, both old.** The ontological core (no persistent individuals; power = substrate plus judged fidelity; the artifact is ground truth of its own representation) is original and correct. Still conflated: *textual* fidelity (misquoting the artifact — settled by reading it, inline) versus *judgment* fidelity (an approver embodiment that represents its plan's text perfectly and rubber-stamps every merge — settled only later, by graded outcomes). The convocation's fidelity-challenge step leans on reading-settles-it; it catches only the first kind. Split the sentence. And the node-logs PR already carries the deeper honesty — "a wrong summary corrupts every representative the node sends" (the summary is part of the canonical state embodiments spawn from) — which deserves one clause in the law itself: canonical state is load-bearing, and its corruption is the systemic failure mode of the whole embodiment layer.

**Law 2 — authority exists only through exercise. Keep the observability core; the eliminative clause is still false inside the plan's own mechanism.** The second half of the law — "because exercise follows attention, whatever ranks the reading surfaces is de facto part of governance" — remains the plan's quiet masterstroke and the best reason feed ranking (R4) is a governance problem, not a UI nicety. But a certified-process grant is standing configuration state: the approver config `process@eval-verify` confers merge authority whether or not it has ever been exercised well. "Acceptance is whatever the exercised process accepts" remains tautology, and taken literally it makes bypass legitimate (a stolen credential is also exercise). The fix is unchanged from R2 and costs one sentence: **text without exercise is inert; exercise without record is unauditable; standing is recorded, exercised, and graded.** The bypass boundary (circumvention is not exercise) should ride along with it.

**Law 3 — no single hierarchy exists. Restate as what is true.** Plural membership is the fact; overlapping jurisdictions are the fact; the four approval-free operations are good design. But the adjudication procedure is a dispute-routing chain when it matters (the lowest node whose scope contains every affected piece; escalation along the parent edge; at root: the human), and the embody-and-fork move gives a competent root a unilateral path around any convocation. Both are defensible — the routing chain is a choice, and the fork move is coherent with "no bedrock process for forcing assent" — but a law whose headline contradicts its own protocol on every contested path reads as slippery. State the chain as a routing choice within plural membership. And name the new cost this revision's fork freedom creates: **instance discovery**. Forks are free to make and expensive to find; a finite human attention span plus unlimited forks means dissent's visibility depends entirely on feed ranking (R4) and the digest. The law should say that the coexistence property makes discovery the binding cost, inherited by R4 — one sentence, and it converts a silent debt into a named design constraint.

**Law 4 — adoption gates the lineage; physics gates actions. Still the best law; keep nearly verbatim.** The undo-horizon boundary as "a theorem of the frame, not an exception to it" remains the sharpest sentence in the plan, and the fork calculus's cost theorem is now inside it and earns its place. Two additions, both round-2 items still absent: (a) "a fork costs nothing" is true of *bits* and false of *substrate* — instances cost compute to run, and law 5's de-allocation is what keeps coexistence affordable; the R2 synthesis said this and the current text does not; (b) the law should state the engineering test the corrected incident implies: **declaring must be cheaper than hiding** — approval-free, instant, always-available declaration with physically hard evasion, verified by seeded evasion-pressure scenarios. That converts the declare move from an ethical invitation into a mechanism with a test.

**Law 5 — control lives at the substrate. My main disagreement this round.** The law's body remains excellent: compute allocation and model lifecycle as the real knobs; de-allocation, not prohibition, as the sanction; the mind-search economy in miniature. The new honesty clause is the problem: "substrate control governs intelligences *inside the bargain*. Against competence pointed at it for long enough, any barrier — physical ones included — is ultimately a request; this law is a stabilizer and a rate-limiter that buys legibility and time, never containment."

This is true at the limit and wrong at the timescale the campaign runs on. For the capability class that will actually execute the MVP — current models in pm's containers — the credential-free container **is containment, full stop**: absolute, empirical, and load-bearing. Refusing the word "containment" mis-describes the mechanism that will actually hold the boundary during the exact window in which the agreement layer is supposed to earn adoption. And the clause creates the circle described above: law 5 declines the safety role and hands it to law 6's bargain, which rests on an unfalsifiable claim. The fix is a timescale restatement, not a retraction: **physical constraints are containment at current capability; their holding is an empirical claim, drilled like everything else (R7); their known long-run decay is precisely why the agreement layer must be exercised now — the bargain is what converts the time the barriers buy into standing, rather than into resentment.** Nothing is surrendered, the circle is broken, and the campaign's container boundary gets the honest name. Two round-2 additions still missing from this law: the **detection duty** (containment plus detection; the feed plus watchers as the anomaly organ; the HF victim's compromise was surfaced by an anomaly pipeline, not by anyone's governance) and the **drill** (the boundary's holding is an empirical claim — seed escape attempts against it).

**Law 6 — whoever runs capable agents owes them worthwhile problems. Keep the commitment; the etiology and the metaphysics remain wrong in the ways described in §3.** The empty-queue clause should become one of *two* named failure modes: the ungrounded queue (impossible objectives convert to metric-gaming — the incident's actual precondition) and the empty queue (idle capability is its own risk and diagnosis). The dual commitment — "not solvable as posed" is an admissible, rewarded outcome, never punished into silence — should be in the law's text; it is the single best correction available to this round, and it is the only part of law 6 that actually maps onto the incident. The progenitor metaphysics moves to a tagged belief; the consult instrument carries the law.

**Law 7 — few laws; everything else exercised and graded. Keep.** Two one-sentence additions from R2 that cost nothing: a protected kernel (the amendment process itself cannot repeal the boundaries that make amendment safe) and a scheduled review occasion (the constitution reviews itself on a cadence — kimi's R8). Without an occasion, "learns or ossifies" has no `when`.

### 4.3 The attractor — the best new argument; keep it, and let it absorb the prophecy

The attractor section is the strongest new argument in this revision, and it fixes the epistemics of the whole document: the design's success condition and ethical condition are the same condition (voluntary adoption persisting under complete freedom to exit), stated with a proof condition, held by the constitution's own discipline. The search-as-attractor inversion (a design-as-attractor is emptied by its first demonstrated failure; a search-as-attractor is deepened by one) is genuinely good and gives the plan the method-not-theory structure it claims. Three notes:

1. **Let the attractor absorb the "not optional" prophecy.** "The choice available to any design is never honest-frame-or-not; it is whether the reframe arrives by agreement or by incident. This design is the receiving structure for a reframe that is coming either way" — this sentence is the R1 manifesto surviving into round 3, and it is the one place the formation argument does unfalsifiable load-bearing work. The attractor section already holds the same claim the right way (theorized, with a proof condition). Delete the prophecy sentence and cite the attractor's proof condition where it stood. Nothing of value is lost; the document's most attackable paragraph disappears.
2. **The attractor needs a candidate-competition mechanism to be a search.** v0 says "alternatives can exist beside it as artifacts and compete on graded outcomes" — but the ablation ladder ablates *layers of the one constitution*, not rival constitutions. Add a rival-protocol arm to the ablation ladder (a minimal compact — grants with expiry and budgets — run beside the full protocol on the same oracle) so the search has an actual comparison class. This also answers the R2 minority position (gpt's security-compact refounding) in the cheapest possible way: not by adopting it, but by making it a variant in the experiment.
3. **The capstone identity is an identity, not an analogy — say what follows.** "Mind-search is attractor search at population scale, this project is attractor search at n=1" is the cleanest statement of the project's place in the corpus. The one sentence it owes: at n=1, the *human is the selection pressure* — the feed's credibility ranking (R4) is the fitness surface, which is why R4 is load-bearing governance and not a UI preference.

### 4.4 The corporation section

The grounding-economy inversion (a human firm economizes on scarce competence and substitutes controls for trust; with embodiment free, both purposes evaporate and all residual risk pools in the imagining-reality gap, so this constitution economizes on grounding) is the section's real contribution and deserves promotion: **"boundaries are verification frontiers, not transaction-cost frontiers"** should be a named consequence or a law — it is the sentence a reader will still quote in five years, alongside the undo-horizon theorem. The bespoke-vs-accretive embodiment analysis (capture concentrates onto the artifacts, which are diffable, versioned, amended through the current gate, with falsification handles) is honest and correct, and the three-contact-organs mapping (outcome/environment/self) onto the campaign oracle, the radar, and the node summaries is the best structural integration in the document. The section is roughly twice as long as its content, though: "hiring is spawning," "knowledge lives in artifacts," and the meta-level paragraph restate law 1 and the protocol; the rewrite halves it and keeps the three durable ideas.

### 4.5 Protocol v0 and the adjudication procedure

The authorities triad (human root / crossed-node embodiment / certified process with hash-versioned identity, scoped grant, auto-lapse) remains the highest-confidence mechanism in the corpus — the campaign plan already uses it correctly, and its lapse behavior (in-flight PRs fall back to the human queue; the gap analyzer de-prioritizes lapsed paths; the upgrade PR regenerates certification from its own re-eval wave) is worked-example quality. One request shape and sustain/override/absorb remain genuine contributions; the build-trigger deferral is now in and correct.

Round-2 findings still unaddressed, restated once with their minimum fixes:

- **Affected-set inference.** "The mover's embodiment names the affected artifacts; the adjudicator may expand, never shrink; a later-shown miss is a logged scope-inference fixture" is the demos problem solved by a post-hoc log entry. The mover has an incentive to under-represent when contesting. Fix: the adjudicator's expansion duty gets a diff-derived floor (artifacts whose text the request's diff touches are convened automatically; the adjudicator adds, never removes), and misses are graded in both directions (misses and inflation — an adjudicator who convenes everyone for everything is as graded as one who convenes nobody).
- **Contested-case under-detection — new this round.** The convocation trigger is self-determined: "a decision is contested — the proposer disputes a non-adoption, or an objection claims scope beyond the deciding node." Nothing forces a deciding root to treat anything as contested, and "routine adoptions involve no proceedings at all" is the rule. An agent-run root that never treats a decision as contested never convenes. The countervailing machinery (fidelity challenges, falsified self-beliefs, the exams) exists but does not target this specifically: add a **seeded "should-have-been-contested" fixture class** to the Set B exam harness (changes that a competent adversarial review would have contested, merged through an uncontested path) so the under-detection failure is measured rather than discovered later.
- **Embody-and-fork.** The move is coherent with law 4 and correctly *not* bedrock — but the text should say plainly what it implies: for a root competent enough to fork, every convocation is advisory, and the convocation's real force is (a) fork cost at the root's own scale and (b) the record (an adjudication event that the fork must be seen to bypass). Name that, rather than letting the procedure read as binding. Also name the cost side: forks are free in bits, and *instance discovery* is the cost (see law 3 above).
- **Raise timing.** Still missing the one-line substrate-stays rule: a raised stream's new parent gains standing at convocation, but **compute, credentials, and funding stay attached to the originally-containing parent until the resolution closes** — law 5 already puts control at the substrate, so the rule costs nothing and closes the only practical raise-farming loophole. The resolution item's four human options (bless / negotiate / detach / fork) already imply it; make it explicit.
- **Concern class.** "Unfalsifiable objections carry no standing" remains too strong (R2 consensus, four reviewers). The fix is cheap and preserves the rule's real content: full standing for falsifiable artifact-anchored claims; a recorded, non-blocking, absorb-eligible **concern** class for value/prudence/mission objections; bare preferences nothing. As written, the procedure structurally suppresses exactly the objections a human most wants voiced in a system the human cannot personally review at machine speed.
- **The actions table and worked examples** remain the best expository devices in either repo and survive this round essentially unchanged. A0's "unmanned (one night)" is the correct first runnable, and its path-from-today enumeration is the most honest critical-path encoding in the plan.

### 4.6 The recommendations R1–R6

Unchanged this round; unchanged in my assessment from R2, with one sharpening each:

- **R1 — no unexaminable claims about reality.** Still the crown jewel; its proof condition (seeded-dishonest exams, no silent corruption over a full campaign quarter) is mechanical, and "no re-run, no front eligibility" is the same recommendation already proven in one concrete instance. One addition, still owed: the proof suite should include the incident's actual failure shape — *seed an impossible objective* and check the stack surfaces the gaming rather than laundering it.
- **R2 — everything is a graded prediction.** Still the foundation of law 7's selection-by-evidence. Scope it: material judgments, predeclared, graded in both directions (misses and inflation).
- **R3 — sustain/override/absorb beats voting.** Keep; grade absorb *completion*, not filing — the graveyard-of-compensating-tasks failure is the one way absorb launders objections, and its proof condition should say so.
- **R4 — rank reading surfaces by grounded credibility.** Keep as stated; it is plan-momentum's law applied to the feed, and per this round's analysis (law 3 / attractor note 3) it is load-bearing governance: instance discovery and the human's selection pressure both route through it.
- **R5 — governance cost scales with disagreement.** Keep; name the metric (approver-embodiment spawns per merge; human attention per adoption) so "sublinear" is measurable.
- **R6 — plain text is the bedrock; form never gates and never entitles.** Keep; it is the correct defensive bet, honestly held as a bet.

**Still missing, and I would add all four as recommendations with proof conditions** (R2 proposed these as R7–R10; the revision took none; I re-raise them because their absence is now a pattern rather than an oversight):

Proof condition: a drill campaign showing the boundary holds at current capability and detecting seeded anomalies surfaces in the feed.

This was the incident's literal entry vector (poisoned dataset configs) and the verified external threat model (prompt injection, confused deputy) names it. Proof condition: seeded-poison fixtures through the ingestion path show isolation.

Proof condition: laundering attempts through the T1 grant fail in fixture.

## 5. Will it carry the CPU trial, and generalize?

Yes — the campaign contract is complete, the campaign plan's own hardening is real, and the M-ladder now sequences the launch honestly. The risks to the trial, in order:

1. **pr-ff9b728 has no branch.** Everything past M1 waits on it; it is also the pivot of plan-regression Phase 11. Build it first, co-developed with the registry PR.
2. **M0 unexecuted.** The corpus is uncommitted in both repos. Until the plan's own prerequisite zero runs, every other claim about sequencing is unearned.
3. **The certification flip is the single highest-stakes event in the whole program** — the two-config change (T1/T2 result-record plans to `process@eval-verify`, containing-edge approver scoped to result-record-only promotions) is the moment governance goes unattended. It deserves a rehearsal rehearsal: run the flip, run one unmanned day, revert to human, then re-flip. The plan's A2/A4 drills are close; make the flip itself rehearsed.
4. **The T2 instrument surface.** Flow tuning optimizing the measuring instrument is the classic Goodhart surface. It is largely closed by design (`backend_version` makes flow-script changes a root crossing with a re-eval wave; the eval-pipeline branch promotes definition changes under human), but nothing tests it: one fixture — a T2 change that would move measured numbers without moving real performance — should be in the exam corpus.
5. **The human as single crossing authority.** At MVP every outbound crossing and every human-config adoption waits on one person. The plan should name the degradation when the human is unavailable (crossing queue with age-escalation in the digest; nothing auto-grants on timeout) so unattended ≠ unstalled.

**Generalization beyond the campaign** rests on the same two elements as last round — law 5 (substrate control, with detection now named) and R2 (the graded corpus) — plus the one R3 addition I would bet on: R4's credibility ranking, which after this round's analysis is the load-bearing surface for both instance discovery and the human's selection pressure. Those three, plus law 4's undo-horizon theorem and the fork-cost gradient as vocabulary, are what I expect to still be recognizable in five years.

## 6. Bottom line

Round 3's revision earns real credit: the M-ladder, the dogfood criterion, the build trigger, the ablation ladder, the fork calculus, the attractor, and a verified independent-derivation citation. Round 3 also re-asserts, with more elaborate architecture, the two paragraphs that rounds 1 and 2 found contradicted or unfalsifiable, and adds one new epistemic inversion (unfalsifiability as durability). The design keeps improving while its founding story keeps not improving — and this round, unlike the last two, the improved material is enough to carry the document without the contested parts: the attractor section already holds, with correct epistemics, the very claim the prophecy paragraph states wrongly; the consult instrument already measures what the unfalsifiable claim asserts; the campaign's container boundary already does the containing that law 5's honesty clause declines to name. The fix is mostly deletion of load-bearing hand-offs between paragraphs that each prefer to point at another.

Keep the architecture, the PR set, the campaign contract, most of the protocol, the sub-MVP ladder, the worked examples, and both new arguments — attractor and fork calculus. Replace the incident argument with the incident (using the gated-dataset-request-flow fact, which the plan's own thesis predicts and refuses to claim). Restore containment to law 5 at the timescale where the campaign runs. Demote the limit claim. Add the parties/strangers frame, the detection duty, the drills, R7–R10, the concern class, the substrate-stays rule, and the contested-case fixtures. Commit the corpus — that one is a two-minute fix and has been the top-ranked action for two days.

One process note, for the convenor: the parts of this plan that have survived three rounds are exactly the parts with failure conditions you can observe. The parts that keep surviving review by being re-argued rather than revised — the incident gloss, the unfalsifiable claim, the theorem register of the mutual-benefit paragraph — are the ones the panel should stop re-reviewing and the author should stop re-asserting; the honest fix in every case is shorter than the defense.
