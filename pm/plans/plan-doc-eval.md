# Document evaluation and prompt improvement

Build and run a document-only improvement loop, then carry measured improvements into PM's existing planning prompts. This is the first bootstrap: generate documents, evaluate their quality, edit the generation prompt and repeat. Code execution benchmarks and full PM evaluation follow afterward.

The broader improvement flow covers all PM use cases, including project import/discovery, automatic plans and recipes, execution/recovery, research, living documents and Radar; see [plan-delivery](plan-delivery.md) and [plan-self-improve](plan-self-improve.md). Document quality supplies the first measure, not proof of downstream usefulness. Keep this bootstrap document-only; add outcome and human-effort measures as those capabilities become runnable.

## Experiment

Use the [collected corpus](../../experiments/plan-writing/sources.json) as known-good writing without manual quality curation. Automatically sample across sources, document types and lengths, so the largest collections do not dominate; keep duplicates and documents from the same project together when splitting development, validation and final evaluation sets.

Every case needs a natural prompt. Assemble each brief from the document's surrounding infrastructure, not from its body: the tracking issue or discussion thread that requested it, the process document or template it follows, the index entry that places it, the working group charter or study call it answers. Record the provenance of every brief. Much recoverable context identifies a document without stating its request: an index entry or metadata header, or a working-group charter or study call shared by many documents. Generation, calibration and training use strong briefs only, those with request text specific to the document, together with PM conversations, spec inputs and sign-off inputs; weak briefs stay out of every split until strengthened by recovering more pre-document context, never by reading the document. Strengthening needs only the catalog and runs alongside the other case work; briefs strengthened before calibration join the strong pool, and later ones join subsequent campaigns. Do not reverse a brief out of the document itself; a brief written from the document trades leaking its content against telling the generator what to write, and no setting of that trade is trustworthy. Prepare continuation cases for every document as well: show the document up to a section boundary, ask for the next section, and hold the original next section as the reference. Documents with recoverable context therefore yield both a whole-document case and continuation cases; documents without it yield continuation cases only. The generator never sees the reference in either case type.

Include PM's own plans as natural-prompt cases. The recovered task conversations are the prompt; the existing plan file is the production baseline, not a known-good reference. Track the quality of regenerated plans against those originals across campaigns, together with fidelity to the human turns. Plans with recovered sessions come first; plans without located histories follow after good outputs rather than blocking the campaign.

Include PM implementation and QA specs in this first loop too. Recover the PR request, plan, notes and code context available when each spec was generated. Implementation cases exclude the later implementation and review findings; QA cases may use the implementation and review findings already available before QA planning, but exclude later scenarios and results. Existing specs, including #235's implementation spec, are production baselines, not known-good references. Judge whether requirements, proposed design choices, unresolved questions and acceptance checks are clear to the reader. Report results separately for plans, implementation specs and QA specs within the whole-document track.

Include PM sign-off reports as a generation task as well. A sign-off report is the `report.html` a sign-off session writes for the person deciding whether to merge: what the PR does, the behaviour verified and its evidence, residual risks and the verdict. Recover the inputs available at sign-off, pinned to the signed-off revision: the prompt, PR request and spec, diff against the recorded base, notes, review and QA results, and the captures the session read. Exclude anything written afterwards and never reconstruct inputs from the report. Existing reports are production baselines, not known-good references. Only about a dozen exist and each re-run overwrites its predecessor, so keep a copy of every new report with a manifest of its inputs from now on. Judge the report's prose rather than its embedded diff and media, and report sign-off results separately within the whole-document track.

Before calibrating judges, survey published quality rubrics for technical and design documents and derive a general, referenced set of quality dimensions with definitions and detection guidance. Place the concerns already identified, padding, premature implementation detail, poor progression and lost requirements, within that set rather than treating them as the whole measure. Preserve useful elaboration rather than requiring every sentence to repeat the input. Judge factual fidelity against the brief or prefix as well as readability. An improved draft may beat its reference; the reference preference assumption bootstraps evaluation rather than imposing a permanent ranking.

Calibrate individual frontier models, cross-lab frontier panels, configured open models and JEV, a general-purpose classifier model reached through OpenRouter, blinded against known-good originals and initial generated drafts. Whole-document cases and continuation cases are separate tracks: scored, calibrated and reported apart, never pooled into one score or paired across tracks. Judges of continuation cases see the prefix. Randomize pair order and hide origin labels. Measure sensitivity to source identity, formatting, length and, for continuations, stylistic continuity with the prefix, which favors the original author. Open-source classifiers such as GLiClass, perplexity and other features that need inference-engine access remain later candidates; ordinary open-model generation and judging are required now.

Separate changes in scoring from improvements in writing. Score the same frozen documents with each panel member alone, repeated judgments from one model, and a panel of frontier models from at least two labs. Match judgment counts to distinguish lab diversity from extra sampling. Panel members judge independently before aggregation; fix membership, aggregation and failure handling after calibration, retaining individual scores and disagreements. Report score shifts, ranking reversals, repeatability, agreement with available human rankings, and uncertainty by rubric dimension and document family. Higher scores or greater consensus alone do not establish a better measure. Record latency and resource use as well as call counts.

Optimize reusable generation prompts on development cases. Whole-document natural-prompt cases are the mode that matches production and the track final selection is made on. Continuation cases reach a wider range of documents, but prompt changes learned from them may transfer less well to fresh plan generation, so whether they help at all is itself a question the campaign answers, through the two-population design below. The evaluator is fixed once the rubric and calibration PRs land; campaigns iterate on generation prompts, not on judges or rubric. Select on validation, and evaluate the selected prompt on untouched cases. Compare against the unchanged production prompt with repeated paired generations and an independent evaluation configuration withheld from the optimizer. Record disagreements instead of hiding them in a single score.

The optimizer is itself a prompt and needs deliberate design. Given a candidate prompt with its generations, scores and judge rationales on development cases, it must separate general guidance, which would help this prompt or any other planning prompt, from fixes that fit only the cases seen; name what in the current prompt probably hurt the scores; and return an edited prompt with that reasoning attached. Version the optimizer prompt with the campaign and keep its reasoning alongside each edit.

Maintain populations of candidate prompts rather than one running best. Use a genetic scheme: fitness is aggregate performance over many development cases, selection keeps the strongest candidates while preserving some diversity, variation comes from optimizer edits as mutation and from recombining sections of strong candidates as crossover. Evaluation is sparse and cumulative, since no prompt will be scored on every case: stored evaluations are never repeated, each generation adds a few new case evaluations per candidate, drawn from cases other than those its edit was derived from, and fitness is the mean of all evaluations the candidate has accumulated, with the evaluation count recorded beside it. Run two populations. The first is evaluated only on whole-document cases and never sees continuation scores. The second is evaluated on continuation cases as well. Cross-breeding between populations is allowed, but a candidate entering the first population is re-scored on whole-document cases alone. Compare the best of each population on whole-document validation and final cases; the difference is the measured benefit of continuations. Record lineage so a winning prompt's ancestry and the edits that produced it can be inspected. Population size and generation count are picked per campaign from the usage estimates described under operation. The diversity-preserving methods noted in plan-self-improve remain later options.

Test iterative generation alongside prompt optimization: draft, critique weak passages, revise, and repeat for a fixed round count. Compare one-shot generation with JEV, individual frontier critics, repeated-single-model critics, cross-lab frontier panels, and open-model critics. Within each writer-model comparison, start from the same draft and hold the prompt, cases and revision procedure fixed. Include open-model writers receiving frontier-panel feedback and an open-model writer/critic combination. Use a bounded matrix selected from calibration, with comparisons at equal rounds and at comparable resource budgets. Score every round with a fixed withheld evaluator outside the critic panel, keeping its feedback out of revision. Report independent quality gains, critic-score gains, disagreements, usage and latency by round, writer and critic. This distinguishes better revisions from simply satisfying a more generous critic. Then combine the two searches: run the genetic search with iterative candidates. Each candidate is a generation procedure in which in-loop judges critique and the writer revises within a single generation; only the final output is scored by the fixed evaluator, and that score alone is fitness. The evolved prompts cover the writer, critics and reviser, seeded from the best fixed iterative configuration and the one-shot campaign winners, and the winner is compared with both at comparable budgets.

Test reader-model generation once the prompt campaign is in place. The writer interleaves reader annotations with the document text as it generates, and the document is extracted from that stream afterwards; only the extracted text is delivered or evaluated. The annotations record how a human reads at each point: what the reader holds in mind and what has faded, which material is salient and which is background, what they are likely thinking about and expecting next, and where the text meets or breaks those expectations. The explicit aim is to help the model inhabit human reading. Models often write text that makes sense yet treats all of it as equally salient, as it is for a model with the whole context in view, while a human reader carries forward only part of what they have read. Less strain on the reader follows: no jarring transitions, unprepared terms or non sequiturs that make a technical document needlessly taxing. Published guidance does not fix the annotation scheme. Gopen and Swan's reader-expectation approach, Flower's distinction between writer-based and reader-based prose, Kintsch's construction-integration model of comprehension and work on LLM-simulated readers, which finds simulated readers understand more readily than real ones, each seed a genetic prompt search run with the campaign's machinery and fixed evaluator, so the search can keep, combine or discard them. A follow-up runs the genetic search with iterative candidates on this approach. Within a single generation, a reviser receives two thought streams: the generation-time annotations, and a post-generation stream from a separate reader model, configured for the document's audience, that reads the extracted text cold. Divergence between the writer's predicted reader state and the reader's reported state measures the writer's theory-of-mind error and locates passages to revise. Only the final extracted output is scored for fitness. The writer, reader and reviser prompts evolve together and are compared with the iterative search winner without thought streams, at equal rounds and comparable budgets. The fixed withheld evaluator never sees annotations or streams; report progression, clarity, concision and requirement coverage in particular, with length changes beside score changes. If the fixed rubric cannot register reader strain, report that rather than adding a measure mid-experiment. Adoption does not wait for this work. Sign-off reports are in scope here; PM's other generated reports, such as QA reports, are later targets.

More experimental still, impose human limits on the simulated reader rather than only asking it to describe them. The aim is to mimic human shortcomings so models communicate better: a reader that forgets and drifts the way people do may show where text fails real readers when an unconstrained model reads it without strain. Research comes first: survey human reading and thinking limits, such as working-memory capacity and what readers carry forward, forgetting and gist retention, fatigue over reading time, and mind-wandering, which in reading rises with text difficulty and is moderated by interest, and turn the findings into parameterized constraints. Two candidates are already in view: extremely aggressive compaction of what the reader has read, so only a gist survives, and injection of unrelated thoughts in proportion to the estimated difficulty or dryness of the passage, so text that cannot recover a lapsed reader shows itself. The constraints are reference targets rather than fixed rules: their parameters join the iterative genetic search, and each stays only if the fixed evaluator's scores of the final documents improve. They apply first to the reader streams, with the writer's own generation as an exploratory arm. Adoption does not wait for this work either.

## Operation and adoption

Reuse `experiments/plan-writing` and the existing `claude-mixed` routes for generators, optimizers, critics and judges. Inventory the configured models when the experiment runs. Give every configured hosted open model a real generation and judging sample, and take representative hosted open models through the iterative comparison and baseline-versus-improved-prompt evaluation; discover them from the live configuration rather than freezing a list here. Locally served models wait for a later PR: this machine lacks the memory to serve them alongside PM work until that work moves to another machine. Current local route aliases include `qwen3.6`, `qwen3.8-27b` and `Qwen3.8-Flash-Next`. Record requested and resolved model/version, lab, backend, local quantization and relevant inference settings, without credentials. A route that is down when a run needs it is reported and the run asks for it to be restored; a route still unavailable after that remains an explicit coverage gap, not a silent substitution or mocked success. Compare gains within each writer model and report transfer of prompt improvements across models. Work that needs inference-engine access, such as logprobs or per-request token accounting, is deferred. Extract production prompt builders only as needed; avoid a general prompt-framework refactor.

Run unattended with checkpoint/resume and a panel decision at campaign checkpoints about whether further work is worthwhile; stop on sustained lack of improvement. This plan sets no compute limits. Each PR that runs models first estimates how much of the weekly subscription limits runs of different lengths would consume, and the run length is picked from that estimate before starting. No single run may use more than twenty percent of any one subscription's weekly limit. Keep prompt versions, task inputs, briefs and their provenance, generated documents, scores, failures and resource use so a claimed gain can be inspected and reproduced.

First adoption targets are the plan-development and PR-breakdown prompts in `pm_core/cli/plan.py`, the implementation/QA spec prompts in `pm_core/spec_gen.py` and the sign-off prompt in `pm_core/prompt_gen.py`. Evaluate the actual prompts used by inline sessions and standalone spec generation where applicable. Test plan prompts on the recovered PM task conversations and public corpus, spec prompts on their recovered phase inputs and the sign-off prompt on recovered sign-off inputs; report each family against its own production baseline. Missing histories do not block the campaign. Preserve user intent, required plan fields, dependency semantics, spec approval and handoff behavior, and the verdict and revision tags in sign-off reports that the dashboard and merge gate read.

Success requires evaluating all the in-scope prompt families and at least one supported improvement to an existing production prompt, followed by fresh generation through its normal PM entry point. Report regenerated plans, both kinds of spec and sign-off reports against their originals, the cross-lab panel's effect on scoring and iterative gains, and open-model results and coverage gaps; local-model results follow in the later local-model PR. Adopt improvements only in families and model configurations where the evidence supports them; retain and report each unchanged baseline. If no candidate wins, the adoption goal remains outstanding. Human review remains available, with no routine manual labeling or approval gate added to the experiment.

A less speculative next step after adoption uses production interaction data. Start recording now how people handle the documents pm generates, so the data accumulates while the campaigns run: each generated plan, spec and sign-off report with its prompt version and model, the edits made before acceptance, approvals, rejections and regenerations, sign-off returns, corrections in notes and answers, and follow-up questions about the document. Human edits and corrections are grounded evidence of what a prompt missed; attention signals such as dwell time can locate where reading broke down, as in the reading feedback loop below, but never count as usefulness. The records then become production cases, with the accepted version as reference, a check on the fixed evaluator, and material for further prompt optimization delivered through the normal adoption path. Records stay local. This overlaps plan-pulse's metrics store and plan-ambient's attention archive; reuse whichever lands first rather than building a third store.

Close a reading feedback loop for velocity as well, since the point of this plan is getting through more technical documents faster. plan-ambient's reading surfaces, the pm rc viewer, the doc reader and later the Even Realities reader, record where the user reads, skims, slows, re-reads and stops. Map that activity onto sections, flag sections where the user seems to have lost interest or lost the thread, and have the plan's PM session propose rewrites of them through the normal revision and approval flow, citing the evidence and preserving every requirement, decision and open question. Reading signals locate trouble; they are not the measure of success. Judge the loop by grounded outcomes: time from opening a document to a decision, fewer revision requests and questions, accepted rewrites and preserved requirements.

The next stage is [execution evaluation](../docs/planning-evaluation.md), starting with existing benchmark inputs and comparing direct execution without a separate plan, current planning and improved planning. It checks whether document-quality gains improve task outcomes. Full PM orchestration and the logged proposal/code/test cases extend that stage; none is a prerequisite here.
